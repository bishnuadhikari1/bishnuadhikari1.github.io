ABOUT

About Me

I am a PhD candidate in the Department of Biological Sciences at the University of Alabama, working in the McKain Lab.

My research uses genomics, population genetics, comparative genomics, and bioinformatics to understand how genomic variation contributes to plant adaptation and invasiveness.

My primary research system is Johnsongrass (Sorghum halepense), an invasive and allotetraploid grass species.

I am particularly interested in understanding how genetic variation, structural variation, polyploidy, hybridization, and genome evolution contribute to ecological success in plants.

RESEARCH

Research Interests

Population Genomics

Investigating genetic diversity, population structure, gene flow, and genomic differentiation across geographic and environmental gradients.

Structural Variation

Characterizing large-scale genomic variation, including inversions, translocations, insertions, deletions, and other structural variants.

Pangenomics

Using multiple high-quality genome assemblies to investigate genomic diversity and variation that cannot be captured by a single reference genome.

Polyploidy & Hybridization

Studying genome evolution, homeologous relationships, hybridization, and introgression in polyploid plant genomes.

Plant Invasiveness

Investigating genomic and phenotypic properties that may contribute to the ecological success and invasiveness of plants.

Bioinformatics

Developing and applying computational approaches for genome assembly, comparative genomics, variant discovery, and population genomic analyses.

FEATURED PROJECT

Johnsongrass Genomics

Genomic properties underlying invasiveness in Sorghum halepense

My dissertation research investigates the genetic and genomic properties of Johnsongrass that may contribute to its invasiveness and adaptation.

The project integrates population genomics, chromosome-level genome assemblies, structural variation, comparative genomics, hybridization, and common garden experiments.

A major component of the work is understanding genomic variation across populations and linking genomic differences with phenotypic variation.
