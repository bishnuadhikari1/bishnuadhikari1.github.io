About

I am a PhD candidate in the Department of Biological Sciences at the University of Alabama and a member of the McKain Lab.

My research uses genomics, population genetics, comparative genomics, and bioinformatics to study genetic variation in plants.

My primary research system is Johnsongrass (Sorghum halepense), an allotetraploid grass species.

Research

Population Genomics

Studying genetic diversity, population structure, gene flow, and genomic differentiation across geographic regions.

Structural Variation

Investigating large-scale genomic variation, including inversions, insertions, deletions, and translocations.

Pangenomics

Using multiple genome assemblies to characterize genomic diversity beyond a single reference genome.

Polyploidy & Hybridization

Studying genome evolution, hybridization, introgression, and homeologous relationships in polyploid plants.

Plant Invasiveness

Investigating genomic and phenotypic properties associated with plant invasiveness and adaptation.

Bioinformatics

Applying computational approaches to genome assembly, comparative genomics, variant discovery, and population genomic analyses.

Projects

Johnsongrass Genomics

My dissertation research investigates the genomic properties of Johnsongrass that may contribute to its invasiveness and adaptation.

The project integrates population genomics, chromosome-level genome assemblies, structural variation, comparative genomics, hybridization, and common garden experiments.
